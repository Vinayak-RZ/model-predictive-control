# Model predictive control for an induction billet heater

**Author:** Vinayak · Industrial Control coursework · IIT Roorkee (Electrical Engineering)

## Abstract

This report compares **offset-free model predictive control (MPC)** against a
**tuned discrete PID** baseline on a CasADi heat-balance twin of an induction
billet heater (IBH). Three scenarios—setpoint tracking, disturbance rejection,
and constrained power—show where prediction and explicit constraints help, and
where a simple PID still competes on energy or raw error if limits are ignored.

All parameters are **illustrative coursework values**, not customer or plant data.

---

## 1. What we built

The project combines:

- **MPC:** SISO offset-free QP solved with SciPy L-BFGS-B on an identified
  linear model `y[k+1] = a y[k] + b u[k] + d_hat`.
- **Plant:** Nonlinear IBH twin `m c_p dT/dt = η P − U (T − T_amb)` stepped in
  CasADi (`twin/ibh.py`).

Additional coursework layers:

## 2. Why MPC for a billet heater?

Induction heating is slow, coupled, and power-limited. A PID loop reacts only
after temperature drifts. MPC uses a model to **anticipate** future temperature
under candidate power profiles, **re-optimises every sample** (receding horizon),
and can treat **power bounds and slew limits** as hard constraints in the optimiser.

The coursework controller here uses a **single held power** across the
horizon (one decision variable). That is simpler than full multi-move MPC but
still captures preview and constraint handling.

**Offset-free extension:** the integrating state `d_hat` is updated from the
one-step prediction error so slow model mismatch and load changes appear as
disturbances the controller can reject faster.

## 3. Experimental setup

- Sample time: **1 s** · Simulation horizon: **180–200 s** per scenario
- Actuator: coil power **0–250 kW** (tighter in the constraint case)
- MPC horizon: **25 steps** (30 in the power-limit scenario)
- PID: position-form with anti-windup; gains per scenario (see `sim/scenarios.py`)
- Metrics: **IAE**, **ISE**, **overshoot**, **settling time** (±3% band),
  **constraint violation steps**, **energy** (∫P dt)

Figures below are generated by `python examples/run_simulations.py`.

### 3.1 Setpoint tracking

Setpoint steps **80 °C → 120 °C** at 30 s.

![Setpoint tracking](../results/setpoint_tracking.png)

| Metric | MPC | PID |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IAE | 1.82×10³ | 2.48×10³ |
| ISE | 4.30×10⁴ | 5.41×10⁴ |
| Overshoot | 2.8% | 0% |
| Settling time | **101 s** | 179 s |
| Violations | 0 | 0 |

MPC reaches the band sooner with lower integrated error; it trades a small
overshoot for faster tracking.

### 3.2 Disturbance rejection

After a setpoint step to **115 °C**, effective heat loss **U increases 45%** at
70 s.

![Disturbance rejection](../results/disturbance_rejection.png)

| Metric | MPC | PID |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IAE | **955** | 1090 |
| ISE | **1.08×10⁴** | 1.36×10⁴ |
| Settling time | **62 s** | 103 s |

Innovation updates to `d_hat` let MPC recover faster after the unmodelled load change.

### 3.3 Power limits and rate constraints

Cap **150 kW**, slew **8 kW/s**, aggressive target **165 °C**. MPC enforces
limits inside the optimiser; the PID baseline is **not rate-limited** (typical
field retrofit) to show the cost of ignoring slew.

![Power constraints](../results/power_constraints.png)

| Metric | MPC | PID |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IAE | 9.48×10³ | 9.15×10³ |
| Violations | **0** | **6** steps |
| Energy | 7.75 kWh | 7.99 kWh |

PID achieves slightly lower IAE only by **breaking the slew limit** six times.
MPC stays feasible; integrated error is similar but operation respects actuator
limits—often the real requirement in industrial power electronics.

## 4. MPC vs PID — advantages and limitations

| Topic | MPC (this extract) | PID |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preview | Uses horizon rollout | None |
| Constraints | Box + slew in QP | Requires ad-hoc clamping |
| Tuning | Horizon, model fit | Kp, Ki, Kd |
| Compute | Small NLP each second | Negligible |
| Model | Needs identified `a,b` | Only gain tuning |
| Offset-free | Built-in `d_hat` | Integrator only |

**Limitations here:** single held move (not full move blocking), linear prediction
vs nonlinear plant, no state constraints on temperature—only on power. A production
deployment would add move penalties, MIMO coupling, and model validation gates
(`model_card.py`).

---

## 5. Conclusion

On the IBH twin, offset-free MPC improves **setpoint tracking** and
**disturbance rejection** metrics while respecting **power limits** without
constraint violations. PID remains valuable as a baseline: lower implementation
cost and competitive energy use when limits are loose, but it falls behind when
load changes or when slew limits must be honoured in the controller itself.

\begin{abstract}
Model predictive control (MPC) looks ahead: it uses a plant model to try future power moves, then picks the best safe move at every sample.
In this report we build a simple offset-free MPC for an induction heater, run it on a CasADi heat-balance simulator, and compare it with a hand-tuned PID and a SciPy-optimised PID on the same tests.
We score tracking with integrated absolute error (IAE) and also count power-limit violations.
After a short retune (let the horizon see the future setpoint, \(N{=}40\), terminal weight \(Q_t{=}50\)), MPC has the best IAE on setpoint tracking (\(1044\) vs \(1466\) vs \(2477\)), under tight power limits (\(8727\) vs \(9033\) vs \(9153\)), and under model mismatch (\(1214\) vs \(1667\) vs \(2541\)), with no limit violations when constraints are active.
Optimised PID is slightly better on two disturbance tests when it is tuned on those same tests.
Overall, MPC is the better choice for this heater: it respects coil power and slew limits by design, recovers faster when the model is wrong, and matches or beats PID on the main tracking cases once it can see the coming setpoint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Induction heaters respond slowly and have hard limits on coil power and how fast that power may change.
A PID loop reacts only after temperature has already moved, and cutting an illegal power command after the fact does not fix the mistake.
MPC uses a model to predict what temperature will do under different power choices, re-solves every sample (receding horizon), and can enforce power and rate limits inside the optimiser~\cite{rawlings2017,camacho2013}.
Figure~\ref{fig:mpc-loop} shows the idea: predict over a short horizon, apply only the first chosen move, then measure the plant and repeat.

We implement that idea with a fitted linear model plus a disturbance estimate for offset-free tracking~\cite{pannocchia2003}, a SciPy solver, a CasADi heater simulator as the truth plant, batch metrics, and a three-screen Streamlit lab.
The physical numbers in this report are illustrative coursework values, not field data.

The practical question is: after we retune the MPC settings (Section~\ref{sec:retune}), and after we let an optimised PID see each test during tuning, which controller should run this limited heater?

\section{Background}
\subsection{Heater plant}
The plant is a simple heat balance on workpiece temperature \(T\):
\begin{equation}
  m c_p \frac{dT}{dt} = \eta P_{\mathrm{coil}} - U\bigl(T - T_{\mathrm{amb}}\bigr),
  \label{eq:heat}
\end{equation}
integrated with explicit Euler in CasADi. Demo parameters are
\(m{=}800\,\mathrm{kg}\), \(c_p{=}480\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\), \(\eta{=}0.82\),
\(U{=}45\,\mathrm{W\,K^{-1}}\), \(T_{\mathrm{amb}}{=}25\,\unitC\).

\subsection{Identified prediction model}
MPC predicts with a one-step SISO ARX model
\begin{equation}
  y_{k+1} = a y_k + b u_k + \hat{d}_k,
  \label{eq:arx}
\end{equation}
with \(a{=}0.978\), \(b{=}0.042\), \(\Delta t{=}1\,\mathrm{s}\) from a 120\,s least-squares step fit on the simulator (\(\mathrm{RMS}\) residual \(0.35\,\unitC\)).
To remove steady-state offset we use the integrating disturbance of Pannocchia and Rawlings~\cite{pannocchia2003}. After the first sample,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{d}_{k} \leftarrow \hat{d}_{k-1} + g\bigl(y_k^{\mathrm{meas}} - \hat{y}_{k\mid k-1}\bigr),
\end{equation}
with observer gain \(g{=}1\) in the retuned controller.

\subsection{MPC cost, constraints, and receding horizon}
Over horizon \(N\) the controller chooses one free move \(u\) (control horizon \(N_u{=}1\) after the sweep in Section~\ref{sec:retune}) and minimises
\begin{equation}
  J(u) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \bigl(y_i - r_i\bigr)^2 + Q_t\bigl(y_N - r_N\bigr)^2,
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where \(r_i\) is the future setpoint seen over the horizon (reference preview).
The chosen \(u\) must stay inside the coil power window and the one-step slew limit:
\begin{equation}
  \max(u_{\min},\, u_0 - \Delta u_{\max}) \le u \le \min(u_{\max},\, u_0 + \Delta u_{\max}).
\end{equation}
Only the first move is applied; then the horizon shifts forward.
We solve the problem with SciPy \texttt{L-BFGS-B}.

\subsection{PID baseline}
The discrete PID is
\begin{equation}
  u_k = u_{k-1} + K_p e_k + K_i \sum e + K_d \frac{e_k - e_{k-1}}{\Delta t},
\end{equation}
with back-calculation anti-windup when the actuator saturates~\cite{astrom2006}.
Hand-tuned gains are the scenario defaults in the repository.
Optimised PID uses SciPy \texttt{L-BFGS-B} to minimise \(\mathrm{IAE} + 250\times(\text{violation steps})\) on the same plant, noise seed, and limits, with bounds \(K_p\in[0.05,12]\), \(K_i\in[0,0.35]\), \(K_d\in[0,8]\).

\subsection{Retuned MPC parameters}
\label{sec:retune}
We ran a short grid on setpoint tracking, starting from legacy settings (\(N{=}25\), \(N_u{=}1\), no preview, \(Q_t{=}0\)) and trying longer horizons, multi-move \(N_u\), terminal weights, move penalties, and observer gains.
Allowing several free moves with a move penalty made IAE worse on this heater.
What helped most was \textbf{reference preview}, a longer horizon, and a terminal weight.
The retuned values used for all results below are:

\section{Experimental setup}
Sample time \(\Delta t{=}1\,\mathrm{s}\).
Default power window \(0\)--\(250\,\mathrm{kW}\) with slew \(25\,\mathrm{kW\,s^{-1}}\).
We use five scenarios:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Setpoint tracking:} \(80\to 120\,\unitC\) at \(30\,\mathrm{s}\) (duration \(180\,\mathrm{s}\)).
  \item \textbf{Disturbance rejection:} hold \(115\,\unitC\); heat-loss \(U\) rises \(45\%\) at \(70\,\mathrm{s}\).
  \item \textbf{Power limits:} cap \(150\,\mathrm{kW}\), slew \(8\,\mathrm{kW\,s^{-1}}\), target \(165\,\unitC\). Hand-tuned PID has rate limiting off (as in the repository scenario) to show the cost of ignoring slew; MPC and optimised PID stay within limits.
  \item \textbf{Model mismatch:} same setpoint test, but plant mass \({+}8\%\), efficiency \({-}6\%\), heat loss \({+}18\%\). MPC still uses the nominal ARX model.
  \item \textbf{Ambient step:} \(T_{\mathrm{amb}}\) rises by \(8\,\mathrm{K}\) at \(70\,\mathrm{s}\).
\end{enumerate}
We also run a robustness check: keep optimised-PID gains from the normal setpoint plant, then switch to the mismatched plant, and compare with MPC and with a PID retuned on the mismatch itself.

\subsection{Power limits and violations}
On the hard \(165\,\unitC\) request with a \(150\,\mathrm{kW}\) / \(8\,\mathrm{kW\,s^{-1}}\) ceiling, hand-tuned PID makes \textbf{six} slew violations and only looks slightly cheaper in energy because those illegal moves are allowed.
MPC and optimised PID stay at zero violations; MPC also has the lowest IAE (\(8727\)).
Figure~\ref{fig:viol} shows the gap.
Ambient-step IAE and settling in Table~\ref{tab:summary} follow the same pattern as Figure~\ref{fig:traces}(b): optimised PID wins IAE after a scenario retune, while MPC still settles faster because \(\hat{d}\) absorbs the load change.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
For a coil that must stay inside a legal power window, the retuned MPC is the better controller.
It has the lowest tracking IAE on setpoint, power-limit, and mismatch tests (Figures~\ref{fig:iae}--\ref{fig:traces}), the fastest settling on every row in Table~\ref{tab:summary}, zero violations when limits bind (Figure~\ref{fig:viol}), and a clear edge when the plant drifts from the model used to tune the PID (Figure~\ref{fig:rob}).

Two IAE rows still favour scenario-tuned optimised PID: disturbance rejection (\(586\) vs MPC \(596\)) and the ambient step (\(578\) vs \(641\)).
Those gains were fitted on the same disturbance the controller then faces, so this is a strong and fair baseline.
MPC still settles faster in both cases (\(46\,\mathrm{s}\) vs \(60\,\mathrm{s}\)) because \(\hat{d}\) absorbs the load change without a retune.
Hand-tuned PID is never best on IAE or settling here; its only ``win'' on energy in the power-limit case comes with six illegal slew steps.

This coursework controller still has limits: only one free move (\(N_u{=}1\)), a linear predictor against a nonlinear plant, power constraints only (no temperature constraint), and illustrative parameters.
A production design would add move blocking, multi-input coupling, and a clearer model check before recommending a move.
The Streamlit lab is for teaching, not a PLC programme.

\section{Conclusion}
For the induction heater studied here, offset-free MPC with reference preview is the controller to prefer.
After the retune it leads on the main tracking and constraint metrics, keeps the coil inside limits by design, and stays ahead of both a frozen and a retuned PID when the plant is mismatched.
Where a scenario-tuned PID still posts a slightly lower disturbance IAE, the gap is small, and that PID needs offline retuning that MPC does not.
The retuned settings (\(N{=}40\), \(Q_t{=}50\), preview on) are the defaults used for the figures in this report.
